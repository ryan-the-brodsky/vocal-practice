## New verification performed (claim 16)
Web-checked the only genuinely new claim against the poor-pitch-singing / amusia literature:
- Congenital amusia prevalence ≈ **1.5%** (rare); up to ~15% *self*-identify as "tone deaf" but most are not amusic.
- Inaccurate singing occurs in ≈ **30%** of the general population.
- Poor-pitch singing can co-exist with **unimpaired pitch perception**; research locates the deficit in
  **sensorimotor translation** (auditory target → vocal motor plan), not in perception.
- Sources: Pfordresher & Demorest (2021), *Journal of Research in Music Education* — "The Prevalence and
  Correlates of Accurate Singing"; "Neurological and developmental approaches to poor pitch perception and
  production," PMC4363098.
- **Decision:** the article states this in general, hedged terms and asserts **no precise statistic**, so no
  new in-article citation was added (matching how the sibling articles handle general FAQ reassurances). The
  claim is verified and safe as written.
