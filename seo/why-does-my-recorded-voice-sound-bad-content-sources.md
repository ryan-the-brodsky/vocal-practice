## New citations added (and why)
- **Maurer & Landis 1990** (PMID 2283129) — grounds the core "bone conduction accounts for the self-vs-recorded gap" claim with a direct experimental measurement rather than resting it solely on the Pisanski self-other-discrimination study. Verified: real paper, Univ. Hospital Zürich, correct volume/issue/pages.
- **Holzman & Rousey 1966** (PMID 5965194) — grounds the "it's unfamiliarity, not badness / voice confrontation" reframe, which is the article's emotional payoff. Verified: real paper, JPSP 4(1):79–86, seminal source for the voice-confrontation phenomenon.

## Fixes applied this run
- **Citation URL corrections:** initial draft had a wrong PMID for Maurer & Landis (2276852 → corrected to **2283129**) and an unverifiable PsycNet record id for Holzman & Rousey (replaced with the verified **PubMed 5965194**). Both fixed in frontmatter + Sources list before shipping.
- **Claim 3 hedging:** the low-frequency mechanism is the mainstream lay/clinical account but the true bone-conduction transfer function is more complex than "just low frequencies," so the draft says bone conduction "tends to carry lower frequencies relatively more strongly" rather than asserting a clean low-pass filter.
