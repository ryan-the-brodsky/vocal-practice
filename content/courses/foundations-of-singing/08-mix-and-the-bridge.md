Most contemporary singing lives in the middle of the voice, where chest and head overlap. That overlap is the mix, and the spot where the voice wants to switch from one to the other is the passaggio, or bridge, or break. Cracks happen there. So do strained high notes and thin, disconnected ones. If you have done lessons six and seven, you now know what each side of the bridge feels like, and this lesson is about crossing it.

Two things to know first. Cracking is normal, including for trained singers, and an occasional crack does not hurt anything. The guide on why voices crack covers that in detail if the cracking has been discouraging you. And there is no single agreed method for building mix. The exercises here come from two different traditions, and that is deliberate.

## What to notice

"Bub" is a descending arpeggio, 8-5-3-1, that starts on the high note and comes down. Entering at the top puts you in head-mix, and the soft "b" keeps the airflow easy, so the descent carries some of that lightness down into chest. The keys start low, climb through the passaggio, and come back down again, so you cross the bridge in both directions.

"Goog" is an octave arpeggio up and back, from the Speech Level Singing tradition. The hard "g" and the narrow "oo" vowel help hold the larynx steady while the line takes you through the bridge. Keep the vowel small; opening it wide near the top is what invites the crack.

In both, the notes to watch on the breakdown are the ones just above where your voice wants to flip. If those notes score wildly off while the rest are fine, that is the bridge showing itself. If they are only slightly flat, you are pulling chest too high. If they are sharp and thin, you have let go too early.

## What done means

Sing both, log them, and find your bridge on the breakdown. You are done when you know which notes it lives on. Smoothing it is weeks of work, not one session, and that is what the routine is for.

Add whichever of the two felt more possible. Most singers find "bub" easier at first because it comes down instead of up.
